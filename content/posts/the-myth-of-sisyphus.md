Albert Camus was a French-Algerian philosopher, novelist, playwright, and Nobel laureate known for developing the philosophy of absurdism and exploring moral responsibility in a world without transcendent meaning. His major works include The Stranger, The Myth of Sisyphus, The Plague, The Rebel, and The Fall. Active in journalism and the French Resistance during WWII, he opposed totalitarianism and revolutionary violence, and took a controversial moderate stance on Algerian independence. Awarded the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1957, Camus rejected the existentialist label, advocating instead for Mediterranean humanism grounded in justice, solidarity, and limits. He died in a car accident in 1960, leaving an unfinished autobiographical novel.
